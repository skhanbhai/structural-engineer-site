Overview / Chimney breast removal / Chimney breasts and the Party Wall Act
Guide · Written by a chartered structural engineer

Do you need a party wall agreement to remove a chimney breast?

Usually yes if the breast is built against the wall you share with next door. Cutting a chimney breast off a party wall is one of the jobs the Party Wall etc. Act 1996 names. So is cutting into that wall to seat a beam or brackets. You serve notice at least two months before work starts. If the breast sits on a wall that only you own, the Act does not apply at all.

Most delays on chimney jobs come from this step, not from the steelwork. Below is how to tell which wall your breast is on, what the notice process looks like from start to finish, why the surveyor will ask for calculations and what to do if you are the neighbour on the other side of the wall.

Section / 01 What it covers

The Act is about the wall, not the chimney.

What matters is which wall the breast is built on and what you will fix into it. Three questions settle most cases.

Which wall is it on?

In terraced and semi-detached houses the chimney breasts are usually built against the party wall, back to back with the breasts next door. In a detached house or on the outer side wall of an end terrace they are on a wall only you own. Only the first case brings in the Act.

What work counts?

The government booklet on the Act lists cutting off projections from a party wall, with removing a chimney breast as its own example. It also lists cutting into a party wall to take the bearing of a beam. A chimney removal on a party wall usually involves both.

Notice or agreement?

People say agreement but the Act works by notice. You tell your neighbour in writing what you plan to do. They either consent in writing or a surveyor draws up an award. Either one lets the work go ahead once the notice period has run.

Three separate permissions. The Party Wall Act, Building Regulations and planning are independent of each other. A neighbour’s consent does not replace Building Control approval and Building Control approval does not replace notice. Planning permission is rarely needed for an internal chimney breast but listed buildings are the exception.
Section / 02 If it is your chimney

Eight steps from first idea to first brick out.

In the order they happen. The figures come from the government’s explanatory booklet on the Act.

1. Confirm which wall the breast is on

Look at both sides. If next door has a breast in the same position on their side of the wall, the two almost certainly share it. In the loft the stack usually rises through the party wall with flues from both houses. Old plans and the shape of the roof help too. An engineer can usually confirm this from photos.

2. Get the support designed first

Calculations before notice. Whether you need a steel beam or gallows brackets decides what goes into the party wall and where. The notice has to describe the work, so it is far easier to serve once the design exists. It also means the surveyor has what they need from day one.

3. Talk to your neighbour

Before anything formal. The booklet recommends discussing the work first so the notice is no surprise. A neighbour who has already seen the plan is far more likely to consent in writing. That can save the cost of surveyors altogether.

4. Serve a party structure notice

At least two months ahead. The notice goes to every adjoining owner, which can include a freeholder as well as the people living there. You can serve it yourself or use a surveyor. Email is only valid if your neighbour has agreed to receive notices that way. The notice lapses after a year, so do not serve it too early.

5. Wait for a reply

Fourteen days. Your neighbour can consent in writing, refuse or do nothing. If they have not consented within 14 days, a dispute is treated as having arisen. In practice that is common and simply moves things to the surveyors.

6. The surveyors agree an award

One surveyor or two. You can both use one agreed surveyor or each appoint your own. Two surveyors pick a third who is only called in if they cannot agree. The award sets out the work, how and when it is done and usually includes a record of the condition of your neighbour’s side before work starts.

7. Budget for both surveyors

Usually you pay. Where the work is only for your benefit, the building owner normally pays for drawing up the award, including your neighbour’s surveyor. Surveyors set their own fees, so ask for quotes early.

8. Start once consent or the award is in place

And the notice period has run. Work can start earlier only if your neighbour agrees in writing. Building Control approval of the calculations has to be in place too. If either side disagrees with an award they can appeal to the county court within 14 days.

Section / 03 If it is next door

Six things to know if your neighbour is removing theirs.

The wall is half yours. So is the stack above it in many terraces. The Act exists to protect you as much as them.

01

You should get a notice

At least two months before work to the shared wall. It should say what they plan to do and when. If builders turn up first, ask them to stop until notice is served.

Two months minimum
02

You can consent or dissent

Consenting in writing is fine for simple work by people you trust. Dissenting is not hostile. It brings in a surveyor to record the condition of your house and set the rules for the work.

Reply within 14 days
03

Your surveyor is usually paid for

For work that only benefits them, the building owner normally pays your surveyor as well as their own. Appointing your own surveyor rather than sharing one is your choice.

Their cost, usually
04

Ask how the stack is held up

If the stack above is shared, their removal changes what holds up your half of it. You or your surveyor are entitled to see the design. A beam or brackets backed by calculations is what you are looking for.

See the calculations
05

No notice is a civil matter

The council does not enforce the Act. If work starts without notice, the formal route to stop it is a court injunction. Most people start with a polite letter asking for notice to be served. A surveyor or solicitor can advise on the next step.

Not the council’s job
06

If it is already done

Ask to see their Building Control completion certificate and the calculations. Photograph your side of the wall and the chimney now. New cracks near the breast, in the loft or around the stack are worth having an engineer look at.

Record your side

2. Calculations for Building Control

Removing a chimney breast is structural work under the Building Regulations. Building Control will want calculations for whatever supports the breast and stack left above. Design is a fixed fee quoted before any work starts. For a straightforward project allow about 3–4 weeks for calculations and drawings.

Planning Portal on Building Control →

3. The same pack for the surveyor

The party wall surveyor will usually ask how the shared wall will be cut and what will bear into it. The calculations and drawings answer that directly. Having them ready before notice goes out is the simplest way to stop the process stalling.

Party walls on GOV.UK →

4. Plan the timetable backwards

Work out when the builder starts. Take off two months for notice and a few weeks for the design. That is when you need to begin. For spring building work, the design wants to start before Christmas.

Section / 05 FAQ

Chimney breast and party wall FAQs.

If yours is not here, send it with a photo. We read every enquiry.

Do I need a party wall agreement to remove a chimney breast?

Usually yes if the chimney breast is built against or into the wall you share with next door. The Party Wall etc. Act 1996 lists cutting off projections from a party wall, such as a chimney breast, as notifiable work. Strictly you serve a notice and the neighbour either consents in writing or a surveyor’s award is made. If the breast is on a wall that only you own, the Act does not apply.

How much notice do I have to give my neighbour?

At least two months before work to the party wall starts. Your neighbour can agree in writing to an earlier start but does not have to. The notice is only valid for a year, so serve it once your plans and calculations are settled rather than months ahead.

What happens if my neighbour does not reply to the notice?

If they have not consented in writing within 14 days, a dispute is treated as having arisen. That sounds worse than it is. It simply means the matter goes to a party wall surveyor or surveyors, who agree an award setting out how the work will be done. You cannot start until there is consent or an award and the notice period has run.

Who pays for the party wall surveyor?

Usually the person doing the work. Where the work is only for your benefit, as a chimney breast removal normally is, you pay for drawing up the award, including the fees of the surveyor your neighbour appoints.

Will the party wall surveyor need structural calculations?

In most cases they will ask for them. The surveyor needs to see how the breast and stack left above will be supported and what will be fixed into the shared wall. The same calculations go to Building Control, so it saves time to have them ready before you serve notice.

My neighbour removed their chimney breast without serving notice. What can I do?

Failing to serve notice is a civil matter rather than a criminal one and the council does not enforce the Act. If work is still going on you can ask them to stop and serve notice properly. A court injunction is the formal route. If the work is finished, ask to see their Building Control completion certificate and calculations. Where you see new cracks or worry about the stack you share, get your side looked at and take advice from a party wall surveyor or a solicitor.

Do I still need Building Regulations approval if my neighbour has agreed?

Yes. The Party Wall Act is separate from planning and Building Regulations. Removing a chimney breast is structural work, so Building Control needs calculations for the beam or brackets whatever your neighbour has agreed.

Do you serve the party wall notice?

No. We are engineers rather than party wall surveyors. We tell you whether your removal is likely to be notifiable and we prepare the calculations the surveyor and Building Control will want. You or a party wall surveyor serve the notice.
